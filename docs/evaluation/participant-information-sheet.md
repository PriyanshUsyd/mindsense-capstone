## What is this study?

You're being invited to try MindSense, a research prototype built by a
student capstone team. MindSense looks at patterns in your own phone usage
(like how far your phone travels via GPS, and how often you unlock it) and
describes how those patterns compare to *your own* recent history — it does
not compare you to other people, and it does not diagnose any condition.

## What will you be asked to do?

You'll use the app for a short session, guided by a facilitator, and try
asking it questions about your own tracked patterns. Afterwards, you'll be
asked for feedback using a structured set of questions covering things like
how clear the responses were, whether you trusted them, and whether
anything felt inappropriate or overreaching.

## What MindSense will never do

- It will never tell you that you have a mental health condition.
- It will never claim your behaviour *caused* something you're feeling.
- It will never give treatment advice or predict your future risk.
- If anything you say suggests you might be in crisis, it will show you a
  fixed, pre-written safety message with real crisis-line information — it
  will not try to generate its own response to that situation.

## Your data

- All processing happens locally, on the device running the study session —
  nothing is sent to any external server or cloud service.

## If you're distressed during or after the session

Please tell your facilitator. They have a fixed script
(`docs/evaluation/crisis-response-script.md`) for exactly this situation,
and can point you to real support resources regardless of anything the app
itself does.

## Voluntary participation

You can stop the session at any time, for any reason, without needing to
explain why.
